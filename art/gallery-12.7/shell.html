Ten hands at Ormgarth

The only identity decision on record is one sentence — “Ten art directions were mocked up and three shortlisted; oil on canvas won” — the ten unnamed and the seven dropped unrecorded. The roadmap also records what that decision was made on: a mockup that drew a hex island and loaded Google Fonts — a fixture, offered as a fact about the game. That mockup is gone and the claim cannot be re-taken, which is itself the argument for what follows. This wall draws nothing of its own.

Where this scene came from

One seeded saga, played to day 261 by the test harness, stopped at the first round where blood had been drawn. Every position, colour, name, proportion and roofline below is read from the game’s own derivations — processionScene, steadingScene, standAt, lookOf — not authored for the picture. The ten panels differ in material and in nothing else.

    The wall

    Ten materials for the same moment: six ranks against four, a man down in the front rank, a winter forest, and eleven roofs of their own steading on the skyline behind. The first panel is the game as it ships — without it there is nothing to prefer anything to.

    What this wall cannot do

    It cannot choose. Naming the direction is yours, and the item’s own “done when” asks the roadmap to record the one chosen and the nine dropped — which is the part the 2026-08-25 decision skipped and the reason we are here again.

    Two directions are not on the wall, and why

    Isometric 2.5DContradicts “drawn side-on”, which is a pillar rather than a preference, and rewrites line.ts — the file that decides a man is the same height in every rank, which is what makes a shield wall read as one.
    Low-poly 3DThat is the Unreal port’s job. Doing it here would build a second renderer to throw away.

    What is honest about the costs above, and what is not

    Two things in the Costs rows are verified: that battle.ts converts screen coordinates to world through an SVG viewBox, so any canvas or WebGL surface needs that path rewritten; and that scripts/look.mjs compares screenshots against a blessed baseline, so every one of the ten — including doing nothing — fails it until a person blesses the new picture.

    Everything else in those rows is an estimate, not a measurement. Node counts, composite cost and “lowest of the nine” are judgements from reading the renderers, not readings taken from an instrument. They are marked that way on purpose: the last time a number about this game’s art was written down as a conclusion, ten roadmap items inherited it.

    The one thing to decide first is not a style. Three of these — pixel, ink wash, and the glyph lattice — take the figures out of the DOM or out of colour entirely. That is a decision about the architecture, and it prices itself: the CSS keyframes, the pointer path, and most of the fifteen browser bars.

    The other seven stay SVG, which means any of them is a change of ink rather than a change of engine, and could be tried behind a flag in a day.
